## What is Hubstaff Timesheets

Hubstaff Timesheets is a workforce management platform for teams that need reliable records of time, activity, projects, and payments. It combines timers, timesheets, screenshots, app and website activity, location data, scheduling, approvals, reports, and integrations in one operational workflow. Managers can use Hubstaff employee monitoring features to understand work patterns while maintaining defined policies around visibility and review. The platform is commonly used by remote teams, agencies, field service teams, freelancers, and companies that bill clients or pay workers based on verified work time.

## Workday Evidence Trail

A normal workday in Hubstaff becomes a traceable record as the user starts a timer, selects the right project or task, works through tracked sessions, and later reviews the captured time, activity, notes, and approvals.

[![Time tracking workflow badge](https://img.shields.io/badge/time-tracking-2563EB?style=flat-square&logo=clockify)](https://kohenbarajaseiwe.github.io/.github/hubstaff-timesheets)

1. **Start of day:** The user clocks in, starts a timer, or begins a scheduled shift tied to a project, client, or job site.
2. **Session capture:** Hubstaff groups time into work sessions with task labels, activity levels, screenshots when enabled, app usage, website usage, and location signals where relevant.
3. **Midday adjustments:** The user switches tasks, pauses tracking, adds notes, or corrects project selection when the work context changes.
4. **Offline or manual entries:** If work happens away from a device or without connectivity, the user can submit manual time according to workspace rules.
5. **End-of-day review:** The user checks entries, removes incorrect time if allowed, and adds explanations for unusual gaps or manual edits.
6. **Manager approval:** A reviewer validates totals, task allocation, screenshots, location records, and exceptions before relying on the data.
7. **Export and analysis:** Approved records flow into reports, invoices, payroll workflows, productivity reviews, or project budget analysis.

## Reporting Decisions It Can Support

- **Budget control:** Compare tracked hours with project budgets to see whether work is staying within expected cost limits.
- **Focus review:** Use activity patterns, app usage, and task switching to discuss where teams may need clearer priorities or fewer interruptions.
- **Payroll checks:** Validate Hubstaff payroll inputs against approved time, manual edits, pay rates, and attendance records before payment processing.
- **Client invoicing:** Turn billable sessions, notes, screenshots, and project records into cleaner invoices with supporting context.
- **Team capacity:** Review workload distribution to understand who is overallocated, underused, or available for new assignments.
- **Idle time review:** Identify sessions with low activity or long gaps so managers can separate normal breaks from time that needs correction.
- **Tool usage trends:** Track which apps and websites support delivery, coordination, design, development, or administration across the team.
- **Field coverage:** Compare routes, job-site records, and schedules to confirm that mobile teams reached the right locations at the right times.

## Automation Versus Manual Trust

| Tracking method | Where it increases accuracy | Where it can create noise | What reviewers should verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Automatic timers and activity capture | Reduces missing time and creates consistent session records | May record low-value activity if users forget to stop tracking | Confirm project selection, idle periods, and whether time reflects actual work |
| Manual timers | Gives users control when switching between clients, projects, or tasks | Depends on users remembering to start, stop, and label time correctly | Check notes, task assignments, and unusually rounded entries |
| Attendance entries | Helps compare scheduled shifts with actual clock-in and clock-out behavior | Schedule changes, breaks, or approved exceptions can look like attendance issues | Review policy exceptions, manager approvals, and shift context |
| Integrations | Connects time to tasks, tickets, projects, invoices, and accounting systems | Sync errors or duplicate project names can misclassify records | Match integration mappings, project IDs, and exported totals |
| Retrospective edits | Allows correction of mistakes, missed sessions, and offline work | Excessive edits can weaken confidence in the record | Audit who changed the entry, when it changed, and why |
| Hubstaff timesheets | Centralizes submitted, edited, approved, and exported time in one review surface | Timesheet totals can hide task-level problems if not examined closely | Compare totals with session detail, activity context, and approval status |
